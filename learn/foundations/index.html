What is waste, really?

Start from first principles. Waste is material that its holder discards or intends to discard, but the same material may retain significant value in another use.

The basics

Waste is a systems problem.

Every day we buy, use, repair, share and discard goods. Decisions made before disposal—product design, reuse, segregation—can matter as much as the final treatment technology.

Four essential ideas

What beginners should know.

01

Waste has different streams

Food scraps, clean recyclable packaging, residuals and hazardous items should not all follow the same handling process.

02

Segregation matters

Mixing organic waste with dry recyclables can contaminate material and reduce its recovery value.

03

Prevention comes first

Avoiding unnecessary waste and extending the life of products is often preferable to treating them later.

04

Measurement matters

Population, consumption, collection coverage and waste composition all influence the size of a waste-management system.

Quick example

What happens to a discarded bottle?

A plastic bottle may be littered, collected as mixed waste, or kept clean and separated. Its eventual fate depends on material type, collection systems, sorting, contamination, economics and the facilities available—not merely its recycling symbol.
